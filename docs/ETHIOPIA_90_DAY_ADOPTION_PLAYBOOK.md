# MERKATO Ethiopia — 90-Day Adoption Playbook

## Positioning
MERKATO should be introduced as a trusted Ethiopian marketplace where people discover local merchants, compare prices, buy with clear ETB pricing, and track orders. Avoid claiming nationwide delivery, verified sellers, or live payment providers unless each is operationally confirmed.

## The first-market strategy
- Start with Addis Ababa as the operational pilot, then expand only when merchant supply, delivery coverage, and customer support are reliable in the next city.
- Recruit a focused set of quality merchants in high-demand categories: phones/electronics, clothing, home essentials, beauty, coffee/food products, and locally made goods.
- Give every pilot merchant a complete storefront: real product photos, accurate ETB prices, stock status, condition (new/used/refurbished), city/area, clear return terms, and response contact.
- Prioritize product quality and fulfilled orders over raw seller or listing counts.

## 90-day rollout

### Days 1–15: Trust before traffic
- Select 20–30 pilot merchants and manually verify their business/contact details using a documented checklist.
- Publish buyer protection, delivery, privacy, prohibited items, and refund/return policies in Afaan Oromoo, Amharic, and English.
- Confirm the actual delivery zones, fees, delivery times, support hours, and escalation contacts. Publish only confirmed coverage.
- Run test purchases with real staff/volunteers across Android devices and low-bandwidth connections.
- Fix failed checkout, missing product images, stale stock, duplicate orders, and confusing order status before advertising.

### Days 16–30: Small controlled launch
- Invite 100–300 early buyers through merchant customer lists, local communities, university groups, and neighborhood networks, respecting consent and anti-spam rules.
- Run weekly product discovery campaigns: local fashion, electronics deals, coffee and Ethiopian-made products, and home essentials.
- Publish short demonstration videos in Afaan Oromoo, Amharic, and English showing how to search, compare, order, and contact support.
- Use the referral programme for trackable invites; disclose reward terms clearly and do not promise cash/discounts until funding, eligibility, and payout rules are approved.
- Contact every first-time buyer after delivery to collect feedback and verified reviews.

### Days 31–60: Repeat purchases
- Measure which categories and merchants produce completed orders, low cancellations, and repeat buyers.
- Promote bundles, wholesale quotes, and group-buy deals only where sellers can fulfil the promised quantity and price.
- Run merchant education sessions: product photography, price updates, inventory accuracy, customer replies, packaging, and dispute prevention.
- Introduce city-by-city campaigns only after courier coverage and customer support are confirmed.

### Days 61–90: Expand carefully
- Add the next city only after the pilot meets its service targets for at least two consecutive weeks.
- Build partnerships with local delivery operators, universities, business associations, creators, and community groups.
- Use real buyer stories and verified seller profiles (with consent) as proof, not fabricated testimonials.
- Review fraud, returns, delivery delays, and support workload before increasing paid advertising.

## Growth loops inside the product
- Buyer invites: simple shareable referral links, clear terms, and fraud-resistant attribution.
- Merchant invites: onboarding links and a guided listing checklist; avoid paying for unverified sign-ups.
- Repeat purchase: saved products, price-drop notifications (only when implemented), reliable order history, and useful reorder prompts.
- Trust: verified-purchase reviews, seller verification status, visible delivery/return policies, and responsive dispute handling.
- Local discovery: filters by city/area and category, with search that works across common Afaan Oromoo, Amharic, and English product terms.

## Metrics to review every week
- Active merchants with at least one in-stock listing.
- Product listing completeness and image success rate.
- Product views to add-to-cart conversion.
- Checkout completion and payment confirmation rate by payment rail.
- Orders delivered on time and delivery cost by city.
- Cancellation, return, dispute, and refund completion rates.
- 30-day repeat purchase rate and referral-to-first-order conversion.
- Median customer support first-response and resolution time.

## Expansion gate
Do not expand marketing spend or open a new city solely because registrations increase. Expand when orders are being fulfilled reliably, merchants keep inventory accurate, buyers understand fees and returns, and support can resolve problems promptly.

## What must be funded or supplied by partners
- Payment provider merchant agreements and credentials.
- Courier coverage, service-level commitments, and approved delivery tariffs.
- Customer support staffing and escalation coverage.
- Any referral/reward budget and a published rewards policy.
- Local merchant onboarding and content production.

## Ethical marketing rules
- Never fabricate ratings, reviews, customer counts, merchant verification, delivery coverage, or discounts.
- Obtain permission before publishing customer stories, phone numbers, photos, or merchant logos.
- Do not send unsolicited bulk WhatsApp/SMS messages; use opt-in lists and provide a clear opt-out.
- Clearly label sponsored listings and promotional prices, including expiry and conditions.
- Keep product availability and delivery estimates honest.
